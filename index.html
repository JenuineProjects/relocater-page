💡 Overall Recommendation

The Netherlands is the stronger overall fit — but Iceland has unique strengths

For your specific situation — an LGBTQ+ couple, two cats, vegetarian diet, Pin1's DEI career ambitions, and Pin2's broad marketing/communications skill set — the Netherlands offers more opportunity and fewer frictions. The job market is larger and better matched to both your profiles, cat import is simple within the EU, vegetarian life is one of Europe's best, and the DEI sector is genuinely developed.

Iceland is not the wrong choice. It offers unmatched safety, world-leading LGBTQ+ acceptance, extraordinary nature, and near-free university tuition. But it demands more: more savings, more patience, harder language learning, mandatory cat quarantine, and a much smaller job market — especially for DEI. It depends on your priorities.

🤝 DEI Sector Opportunities

This is arguably the most important career comparison for you. The difference between the two countries is stark.

🇮🇸 Iceland — DEI Landscape
DEI as a standalone career path essentially doesn't exist. Perhaps 1–3 specialist positions open in the entire country at any time.
World-leading gender equality framework — topped the WEF Gender Gap Index for 14+ years. Equal pay certification (Jafnlaunavottun) is a genuine innovation.
"DEI" is folded into broader HR roles. Dedicated "Head of DEI" positions don't appear on Icelandic job boards.
  • Possible employers: Jafnréttisstofa (Directorate of Equality), City of Reykjavík, University of Iceland — roles are rare and require Icelandic
  • Growing need around multicultural integration as immigration increases, but framed as "integration" not "DEI"
  • You'd be a pioneer, which is either exciting or exhausting
🇳🇱 Netherlands — DEI Landscape
One of the most developed DEI markets in continental Europe, and growing thanks to EU CSRD/ESG requirements.
Multinationals with dedicated DEI teams: Shell, Unilever, Philips, ASML, Booking.com, Adyen, ING, ABN AMRO, Heineken — nearly all English-speaking.
DEI consultancies: Include, Diversiteit in Bedrijf, Equileap, plus Big Four DEI practices.
  • Workplace Pride — international org focused on LGBTQ+ workplace inclusion, runs Global Benchmark. Direct career target for you
  • COC Nederland does corporate engagement and training
  • Movisie — national knowledge institute for social issues, research and advisory roles
  • Salary: €50,000–€75,000 for corporate DEI roles
Dutch DEI conversation can lag behind Anglo-American maturity — you may educate as much as implement. But that's opportunity.
💼 HR Career Prospects
🇮🇸 Iceland

Tight labour market (~3–5% unemployment). HR professionals needed at banks, tech companies, airlines.

Almost all HR roles require fluent Icelandic — employment contracts, union negotiations (~90% membership), labour law.
Without Icelandic, limited to international/tech companies — but these exist (Marel, Controlant, Icelandair, CCP Games).

Salary: ISK 550,000–850,000/month (€3,600–€5,600). Senior roles to ISK 1M+ but require Icelandic + local network.

Key boards: Alfred.is, Tvinna.is, Starfatorg.is, LinkedIn

🇳🇱 Netherlands

Large, mature HR market. Amsterdam, Eindhoven (Brainport), and Utrecht are strongest.

English-only HR roles exist at international companies, scale-ups, and multinationals.
Pure HR generalist roles at Dutch companies require Dutch. Works councils (ondernemingsraad) operate in Dutch.

Salary: €45,000–€65,000 mid-level HR/People Analytics. Senior higher, especially in tech.

Key boards: LinkedIn, iamsterdam.com/work, Indeed.nl

🤖 AI for Business / HR Tech

Your MSc in AI for Business is a genuine differentiator in both countries — but the market size differs enormously.

🇮🇸 Iceland

Companies are actively digitising and HR tech / people analytics demand outstrips local supply. But you might find only 2–5 relevant openings at any given time in the entire country.

🇳🇱 Netherlands

"People Analytics" roles are growing and more likely to be English-friendly. Your sweet spot: a DEI role at a multinational that values the AI/data angle — measuring inclusion, bias in hiring algorithms, diversity metrics dashboards. This intersection is niche, in demand, and pays well.

🐱 1. Your Cats

This is where the two destinations diverge most dramatically. If you want the short version: bringing cats to the Netherlands is simple; bringing them to Iceland is a major undertaking.

🇮🇸 Iceland — Complex & Emotional
Mandatory 4-week quarantine at Keldur quarantine facility. Restricted visits. No exceptions.
Cost: ~€1,260–€2,140 per cat (total for 2 cats: €2,520–€4,280)
Start process 6 months before move. Apply to MAST for import permit.
  • Rabies vaccine (60+ days before travel, max 12 months)
  • Feline distemper, rhinotracheitis, calicivirus, FeLV vaccinations
  • Blood tests for antibody levels (allow 2–4 weeks for lab results)
  • Internal + external parasite treatments (specific timing windows)
  • Vet health certificate within 10 days of travel
  • Only Keflavík Airport allowed as entry point
Emotional reality: your cats will be in an unfamiliar facility without you for a month. Visits restricted. Some cats cope; others are significantly stressed.
🇳🇱 Netherlands — Straightforward
EU Pet Passport — standard intra-EU process. No quarantine.
Cost: ~€180–€360 per cat (total for 2 cats: €360–€720)
Process takes just 4–6 weeks from first vet visit.
  • Confirm microchips (likely already done if registered in Ireland)
  • Rabies vaccination (21+ days before travel)
  • EU Pet Passport from Irish vet
  • Tapeworm treatment if required (check NVWA for latest)
Ferry option: Irish Ferries Dublin→Holyhead, then drive/Eurotunnel. Pet-friendly cabins available (~€20–€40 supplement). Avoids airline stress entirely.

Once there: register with local vet (dierenarts). Cats commonly kept indoors in Dutch apartments. Pet insurance (huisdierenverzekering) available for unexpected vet bills.

🛡️ Safety
🇮🇸 Iceland is measurably safer — near-zero anti-LGBTQ+ incidents. Fewer than 5 reported LGBTQ+-targeted incidents in the entire country in 2024. Vanishingly rare.

Both are among the safest places in the world to be queer. The Netherlands is still considerably safer than most countries — but Iceland is in a class of its own.

🏛️ Tax Implications of Leaving Ireland
  • File Form P85 with Irish Revenue (revenue.ie) to notify of departure
  • You remain "ordinarily resident" for Irish tax purposes for 3 full tax years after departure — double taxation treaties prevent actual double taxation
  • Obtain a U1 form from Department of Social Protection (social insurance record)
  • Keep Irish bank account open for 6–12 months for trailing payments
  • Both countries have double taxation treaties with Ireland
  • Set up a Wise account before leaving — best rates for international transfers, bridges the gap before local bank setup
🌿 5. Lifestyle & Culture
🤝 Social Life & Making Friends

🇮🇸 Iceland

The honest truth: Icelanders form social groups in childhood and they tend to stay closed. You'll likely socialise primarily with expats initially. It takes 2–3 years to develop genuine Icelandic friendships.

Best social shortcut: The swimming pool hot pots (sundlaugar). Go regularly — this is where Icelanders actually socialise. Also: sports clubs, choir, volunteer work, hobby groups.

Reykjavík punches above its weight culturally — legendary music scene, Harpa concert hall, numerous galleries, Iceland publishes more books per capita than anywhere on earth.

🇳🇱 Netherlands

Same problem, different flavour: Dutch people also have established friend groups. The infamous "agenda culture" (agendacultuur) — coffee scheduled 3 weeks in advance. Takes 2–3 years here too.

Best strategy: Join a sportvereniging (sports club), attend borrel (drinks gatherings), volunteer. Structured social settings are how Dutch adults make new friends too.

Large, active expat community, especially in Amsterdam, The Hague, and Eindhoven. Meetup groups, InterNations. Risk: getting stuck in the expat bubble.

Dutch directness: "Not good" means not good. Coming from Irish indirectness, this can feel shocking. It's not personal — it's just Dutch.

🗣️ Language

🇮🇸 Icelandic

Hard. Four cases, three genders, complex verb conjugations. Among the hardest European languages for English speakers.

Not needed for daily survival (most Icelanders under 60 speak excellent English), but essential for career advancement and genuine social integration.

Budget 1–2 years for functional conversational; 3–5 years for professional fluency. Free course: icelandiconline.com (University of Iceland). Also: Mímir, Tin Can Factory, Multicultural Centre.

🇳🇱 Dutch

Easier for English speakers — same Germanic family, many cognates.

Almost every Dutch person speaks English — blessing (you survive) and curse (they switch to English the moment they detect your accent, making practice almost impossible).

🌤️ Weather & Mental Health

🇮🇸 Iceland

Winter: 4–5 hours of usable daylight (Dec: sunrise ~11:15, sunset ~15:30, barely clears horizon). Dark when you leave for work, dark when you come home. Relentless, bone-cutting wind.
Summer: Near 24-hour daylight. 10–18°C. The landscape transforms. This is when Iceland earns its keep.
SAD is real and widespread. Vitamin D supplements, light therapy lamps. The first winter breaks many newcomers. If either of you has any history of depression or anxiety, take this extremely seriously.
Extraordinary nature — glaciers, volcanoes, hot springs, waterfalls, northern lights. Pristine air quality. Geothermal swimming pools are genuine joy.

🇳🇱 Netherlands

Grey: 200+ overcast days/year. Constant wind. Flat grey sky meeting flat grey land at the horizon.
Winter is dark but not Icelandic dark — sunrise ~8:45, sunset ~16:30 in Dec. Significantly more daylight. SAD is common but less extreme.
Winters 0–5°C, not extreme. Summers can be lovely (20–28°C) but heatwaves increasing (35°C+), most homes lack AC.

Coming from Ireland: roughly comparable weather. Slightly colder winters, slightly warmer summers, similar greyness. The winter darkness difference vs Iceland is significant for mental health.

World-class cycling infrastructure. You will cycle everywhere — to work, shops, pubs, in the rain, in a suit, carrying groceries. It becomes your default transport and one of the best things about living here. Buy a sturdy omafiets (€100–€200 second-hand). Use two locks — theft is a national sport.
