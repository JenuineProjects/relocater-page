📌 Quick Summary

Based on the combined analysis of all four experts, the Netherlands emerges as the stronger overall choice for your specific situation — primarily due to the much simpler cat import process, a larger and more English-friendly job market (especially for Pin2's NGO focus), lower startup costs, and the 30% tax ruling that could save thousands annually. 🌈 Social Attitudes & Daily Life

🇮🇸 Iceland

Overwhelmingly accepting. Reykjavík is a city where LGBTQ+ people are visible, normalised, and unremarkable. Holding hands, a quick kiss — nobody cares. Anywhere.

Near-zero hate crime incidents. Uniform acceptance across generations and geographies. You'll likely never have to think about your safety as a queer couple.

The most common microaggression is the assumption of heterosexuality — people assuming you're friends/flatmates. Not hostile, but occasionally tiring.

🇳🇱 Netherlands

Broadly excellent in the Randstad (Amsterdam, The Hague, Rotterdam, Utrecht). Long, proud queer history. Amsterdam is one of Europe's historic LGBTQ+ capitals.

Acceptance is not entirely uniform. Some urban neighbourhoods and the Bible Belt region can be less comfortable. Verbal harassment incidents have been increasing in recent years (170+ reported in Amsterdam alone in 2023).

The Netherlands has a "tolerance paradox" — pride in being progressive can mask ongoing issues. Day-to-day in the Randstad: you'll be fine. But slightly more situational awareness is needed than in Iceland.

🇮🇸 Iceland — Housing

Severe housing shortage in Reykjavík. No regulated private rental market. Most rentals found informally via Facebook groups (Leiga á Íslandi) or Mbl.is classifieds.

Expect to compete with dozens of applicants. Landlords prefer Icelanders with local references. Deposit: 1–3 months' rent.

Pet-friendly rentals: Most Icelandic landlords are relatively relaxed about cats, but confirm in writing.

Buying: EEA citizens can buy. 60–80m² in Reykjavík: €300,000–€500,000. Need 20–25% down payment. Mortgage rates: 7–9% (indexed loans).

🇳🇱 Netherlands — Housing

Housing crisis is real. Social housing waiting lists: 8–15 years (Amsterdam). You'll use the private market.

Scams are rampant. Never pay before viewing. Never wire to foreign accounts. Use Pararius, Funda only.

"Unfurnished" often means literally unfurnished — no flooring, no lights, sometimes no kitchen. Budget €2,000–€4,000 to fit out.

Pet-friendly: Not all landlords accept pets. With 2 cats, options narrow. Some charge extra.

Landlords require income proof: typically 3–3.5× monthly rent as gross salary. Two incomes help.

💡 Tips for Finding Housing from Abroad

  • Start looking early — 2–3 months before your move at minimum
  • Book temporary accommodation (Airbnb, 2–4 weeks) so you have a base for viewings
  • Iceland: Join Facebook groups "Leiga á Íslandi" and "Leiguskjól"; check Mbl.is classifieds
  • Netherlands: Use Pararius.nl (expat-friendly), Funda.nl. Avoid Facebook marketplace (scam-heavy)
  • Mention your cats upfront to avoid wasting time on non-pet-friendly rentals
  • Having a local contact who can vouch for you or attend viewings is enormously valuable

🧾 Tax Implications of Leaving Ireland

  • Notify Revenue: File Form P85 via revenue.ie when you leave
  • Split-year residency: You'll be Irish tax resident for the portion of the year you were in Ireland
  • Ordinarily resident: You remain ordinarily resident for 3 full tax years after departure
  • Double taxation treaties: Ireland has treaties with both Iceland and the Netherlands — you won't be taxed twice
  • Get a U1 form from the Dept of Social Protection to certify your social insurance record
  • Keep your Irish bank account open for 6–12 months — trailing payments, tax refunds, etc.

🌍 5. Lifestyle & Culture

🇮🇸 Iceland — Lifestyle

The Good

Absurdly safe. Virtually no violent crime. Walk anywhere, any hour.

Extraordinary nature. Glaciers, volcanoes, hot springs, waterfalls. Geothermal swimming pools (sundlaugar) are a cornerstone of social life.

Clean air, geothermal everything. Pristine environment. Almost no air pollution.

Good work-life balance. 36–40 hour work week. Generous leave policies.

The Hard

Winter darkness: 4–5 hours of usable daylight Dec–Jan. SAD is real and widespread. This has broken many newcomers.

Isolation: Flights to Europe take 3+ hours, cost €200–600 return. You will feel far from family.

Icelandic is hard. 4 cases, 3 genders, complex conjugations. 1–2 years for conversational level, 3–5 for professional fluency.

Making friends: Icelanders form social groups in childhood. Expect 2–3 years to build genuine friendships. First social circle will likely be expats.

Cost shock: €14 pint, €20 pizza, €80 weekly shop for two. Relentless.

🇳🇱 Netherlands — Lifestyle

The Good

World-class cycling infrastructure. Dedicated bike lanes everywhere. You'll cycle to work, shops, pubs. It's genuinely wonderful.

Excellent public transport. Trains, trams, buses connecting all cities. OV-chipkaart system.

Good work-life balance. Part-time work normalised. 25 days vacation common. Fridays off is a thing.

Rich cultural life. World-class museums, thriving music scene, excellent nightlife.

Same currency (EUR). No exchange rate risk coming from Ireland.

The Hard

Weather is grey. 200+ overcast days/year. Similar to Ireland but with colder winters. SAD is common.

Dutch directness. Your manager will tell you bluntly your work isn't good. Coming from Irish indirectness, this can feel shocking.

Making Dutch friends is hard. "Agenda culture" — coffee is scheduled 3 weeks in advance. Social circles form in childhood. 2–3 years to build real friendships.

Everything closes early. Shops close at 18:00. Birthday parties involve sitting in a circle eating one piece of cake.
